About Me

My interest in communication began with everyday conversations: listening to people's stories, understanding their feelings, and noticing how differently people express themselves. During my undergraduate studies, that curiosity developed into an interest in empirical communication research. I have since worked on survey-based studies of emoji use and intergenerational communication, and I am continuing to learn new methods for studying digital communication.

Research direction

I hope to examine how AI assistance affects emotional expression, willingness to communicate, and relationships between people, including across generations.

Selected Research

First Author · Undergraduate Final-Year Project

Beyond Technological Connection

Perceived Social Media Affordances, Affective Empathy, and Young Adults' Willingness to Communicate with Parents · 2025–2026

Examined how perceived accessibility, editability, and information retrieval relate to young adults' willingness to communicate with parents, with affective empathy as a potential mediator.

430 valid survey responsesCFASEMSPSS & R

My role: First author and group leader; collaborated on literature review and survey design and took primary responsibility for data analysis.

Key finding: Accessibility showed a positive indirect association with communication willingness through affective empathy, while editability showed an unexpected negative association.

Research findings describe associations, not causal effects. Manuscript status should be confirmed before public posting.
First Author · Quantitative Research

The Effects of Personality and Mental Health on Emoji Usage Frequency

2024–2025

Investigated associations among personality groups, mental health measures, and frequency of emoji use in online communication.

898 valid survey responsesFactor analysisANOVAMediation analysis

My role: Worked with teammates on the research process and used SPSS for statistical analyses.

Key finding: Emoji use differed across personality groups; mediation through mental health measures was observed for some groups but not others.

Earlier work submitted to an NCA conference; no publication or acceptance is claimed here.
